# Omikuji Stand — English Script

Guía rápida para el amigo que atiende el stand. El libreto está en inglés; las notas entre corchetes son para él.
La idea: **no esperar a que la gente llegue, sino narrar tan alto y tan divertido que la gente se acerque sola.**

---

## 1. The call — rotate every few minutes

> "Free fortunes! Your luck is hiding in this bamboo box!"

> "Step right up — one shake and your future is on paper!"

> "Who wants to know their luck today? It's free!"

> "Thirty seconds. One shake. One fortune. Who's brave?"

> "Great luck or small luck? Come find out!"

> "Japanese fortune telling, right here! No app, no sign-up!"

[De pie, sonriendo, mirando a los ojos. Decir la línea al grupo, no al celular. Cambiar de frase cada 2–3 minutos.]

---

## 2. The hook — when someone slows down

> "You! Yes, you — do you want good luck or great luck?"

> "Have you ever had a Japanese fortune? It takes thirty seconds."

> "One question: are you feeling lucky today?"

[Señalar a una persona concreta. Una pregunta engancha más que "Do you want to play?".]

---

## 3. The explanation — 15 seconds, simple words

> "This is an **omikuji**. In Japan, people go to a temple, shake a bamboo cylinder, and get a paper fortune.
> Today, your phone is the cylinder. You scan this code, shake your phone, and your fortune appears.
> It's free. No app, no sign-up."

[Mostrar el QR a la altura del pecho. Si ya está escaneando, no seguir explicando: dejarlo jugar.]

---

## 4. The invite — hand them the moment

> "Scan it with your camera. Go on — I'll wait."

> "Now think of one wish. Don't say it out loud, or it won't come true."

> "Ready? Shake it! Shake it like you mean it!"

[Durante los 1.5 segundos de animación:]

> "Shake, shake, shake… here it comes…"

---

---

## 6. The close — keep the line moving

> "Take a photo — it's your fortune for the day."

> "Who's next? One shake, one fortune!"

> "Bring a friend — their luck might beat yours."

> "Come back later. Every draw is different."

[Después de cada reveal, mirar a otra persona y decir: "You're next."]

---

## 7. If they hesitate

> "No pressure — watch one. It takes twenty seconds."

> "It's free. Worst case, you get good news."

> "Just the shake. I'll do the talking."

---

## 8. Short version — peak hour (10 seconds)

> "Free Japanese fortune! Scan, shake, read. Thirty seconds. Who's next?"

[Cuando hay fila, no explicar: escanear, agitar, reaccionar, siguiente.]

---

## 9. Crowd magnets — how to make them come by themselves

1. **Never be silent.** Narrate the current player loudly. Silence kills the stand.
2. **Seed the crowd.** A friend plays first. Two people at the stand look like an event.
3. **Point and invite.** After each reveal, pick one person: "You're next."
4. **Ask, don't pitch.** "Good luck or great luck?" gets an answer. "Want to play?" gets a no.
5. **Keep a rhythm.** One player every 30–40 seconds. The queue itself attracts more people.
6. **Hold the fortune up.** React with the paper/phone high so passersby see it.
7. **Use names.** "What's your name? Maria! Let's see Maria's luck."
8. **Make them read the advice out loud.** People linger and laugh; a small crowd forms.
9. **Photo moment.** "Hold it up — photo!" Others see the screen and get curious.
10. **Two magic words:** "It's free."

---

## 10. Body language

- Stand up, don't sit.
- Speak to the group, not to the phone.
- Big reactions; small reactions make a small crowd.
- Never grab the visitor's phone. Let them hold it.
- Keep the QR visible at chest height.
- Smile before you speak. It changes how the line lands.

---

## Pronunciation for the friend

- 大吉 **Daikichi** — dah-ee-KEE-chee
- 中吉 **Chūkichi** — CHOO-kee-chee
- 小吉 **Shōkichi** — SHOH-kee-chee
- 吉 **Kichi** — KEE-chee
- 末吉 **Suekichi** — soo-eh-KEE-chee

No need to be perfect. Enthusiasm beats pronunciation.

---

## If the visitor speaks Spanish

> "¿Quieres tu fortuna? Es gratis. Escanea, agita el teléfono y listo."

Then switch back to English for the reveal — the reaction is physical, it works in any language.
